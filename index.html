Engineering

Everything XB-1 proved, scaled up.

In January 2025, XB-1 became the first independently developed jet to break the sound barrier. Its carbon fiber composites, digitally-optimized aerodynamics and supersonic intakes are the foundation Overture is built on.

Materials

Carbon fiber, ply by ply.

Each layer is laid at its own angle (0°, 45°, 90°) so the airframe carries load along the paths it actually flows, at a fraction of the weight of metal.

Down to the Silicon

Digital to the core.

XB-1 flew with an augmented reality vision system and aerodynamics refined in simulation. Overture carries those learnings forward, with computation at the heart of a supersonic airliner.

Supersonic

Shock cone half-angle μ = asin(1/M) = 36.0°

Over water, Overture cruises at Mach 1.7: twice the speed of today's airliners, cutting flight times in half across more than 600 global routes.

Boomless Cruise

The boom that never lands.

Using Mach cutoff physics, the sonic boom refracts upward through the atmosphere, the way light bends in water, and never reaches the ground. Over land, that means flights 40–50% faster. San Francisco to New York in 3 h 25 min.
